Question answer

Do I need a demolition permit in WA?

Short answer

Usually yes, on form BA5. The exception: a patio, shed or pool of 40 m² or less, with no asbestos, on a property that isn't heritage-listed. A BA5 for a house or Class 10 structure costs $121 plus the Building Services Levy.

Last updated 4 November 2026

When you need a demolition permit

In WA, pulling down a building usually needs a demolition permit from your council, applied for on form BA5. There's a state exemption for small backyard structures:

  • a patio, shed or pool of 40 m² or less doesn't need a demolition permit
  • as long as it has no asbestos
  • and the property isn't heritage-listed

Anything bigger, anything with asbestos, and any house needs a BA5.

What it costs

ChargeAmount
BA5 for a house or Class 10 structure (shed, garage, carport, patio)$121

These fees are set by the state, so they're the same at every council.

Demolition and planning approval

A demolition permit is a building approval. If the property is heritage-listed or in a heritage area, check with your council's planning team first, as the exemption doesn't apply and other approvals may be needed.

Removing a structure that was never approved

If you're pulling down a shed or patio that never had a permit, demolishing it is one way to fix the problem. The same 40 m² exemption applies. If you'd rather keep it, see retrospective approval.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a permit to demolish a shed in WA?

Not if the shed is 40 m² or less, has no asbestos and the property isn't heritage-listed. Otherwise you need a demolition permit (form BA5) from your council.

How much is a demolition permit in WA?

$121 for a house or Class 10 structure like a shed, garage or patio, plus the Building Services Levy. The fee is the same at every council.

Which form do I use for a demolition permit?

Form BA5, Application for demolition permit, lodged with your council.

Can I demolish a patio without a permit?

Yes, if it's 40 m² or less, has no asbestos and the property isn't heritage-listed.
