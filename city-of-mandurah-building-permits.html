City of Mandurah building permits

Short answer

In the City of Mandurah, new houses, garages, carports, pools and granny flats always need a building permit. Small freestanding sheds and patios (up to 10 m² and 2.4 m) and pergolas (up to 20 m²) don't. Planning approval usually isn't needed if you meet the R-Codes.

Last updated 26 November 2026

Planning approval

For most house projects, planning (development) approval may not be needed if you meet the R-Codes deemed-to-comply rules. It depends on your zoning, site cover, setbacks and any special conditions. The City offers a deemed-to-comply check (a fee applies) to confirm in writing before you start.

Some special cases:

  • Pergolas and shade sails: generally no planning approval, unless you're on the canals.
  • Pools: generally no planning approval, unless you're in a heritage area. Pool fencing may still need it.
  • Demolition: taking down a shed, patio or pergola of 40 m² or less doesn't need a demolition permit, as long as it's not heritage listed and there's no health or safety risk.

How to apply

Use a BA1 (certified by a private building surveyor) or a BA2 (checked by the City). The City prefers online lodgement through "I want to" then "Building Lodgements" on its website. Building Services is on 9550 3777.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a building permit for a carport in Mandurah?

Yes. The City of Mandurah says a building permit is always required for a carport and a garage.

What can I build without a permit in Mandurah?

Small freestanding sheds and patios up to 10 m² and 2.4 m high, pergolas up to 20 m², low retaining walls up to 0.5 m, compliant fences and rooftop solar, among others.

Does Mandurah have a deemed-to-comply check?

Yes. For a fee, the City will confirm in writing whether your project meets the R-Codes and so doesn't need planning approval.

How do I lodge a building permit in Mandurah?

Online through the Building Lodgements link on the City's website, or on paper if you can't lodge online.
