Development approval vs building permit

Short answer

They're two separate approvals. Development (planning) approval checks whether the design and location suit the planning rules. A building permit checks it's safe and meets the building code. If you need both, get planning approval first. Many small projects only need a building permit.

Last updated 9 November 2026

Two different approvals

In WA, "council approval" can mean two separate things. Many projects need one, some need both, and some need neither.

Development (planning) approvalBuilding permit
What it checksWhether the design and location suit the zoning and planning rules: setbacks, height, size, looks, overlooking, heritageWhether it's safe and meets the building code: structure, fire separation, wind, drainage
Main rulesThe R-Codes, the council's planning scheme and local policiesThe Building Act 2011 and the National Construction Code
When you need itWhen your project doesn't meet the "deemed-to-comply" rules, or is in a heritage area or special zoneFor most building work, except small exempt structures
ApplicationCouncil development application form, plans, a cover letter and the certificate of titleForm BA1 (certified by a private surveyor) or BA2 (checked by council)
Usual timeframe60 days, or 90 days if neighbours or agencies are consulted10 business days (BA1) or 25 business days (BA2)
Typical fee$147 for work under $50,000 (as the City of Melville lists it)0.19% (BA1) or 0.32% (BA2) of the value, at least $121, plus the Building Services Levy

Which comes first?

Planning approval comes first. The City of Belmont says a building permit can't be issued until any required development approval has been granted and its conditions resolved. Some councils, like Wanneroo, let you lodge an uncertified building application while the planning one is pending, but it's put on hold until planning approval is issued. A certified (BA1) application must include all prior approvals.

Councils vary, so check your council's rules.

Exempt doesn't mean anything goes

Being exempt from one approval doesn't exempt you from the other. Belmont puts it plainly: a structure may be exempt from a building permit but still need planning approval, or the other way around. And even exempt work must still meet the building standards.

Questions

Common questions

What is the difference between development approval and a building permit in WA?

Development (planning) approval checks the design and location against zoning and planning rules. A building permit checks that the building is safe and meets the building code. They're separate approvals.

Do I need development approval before a building permit?

Yes, if your project needs both. A building permit can't be issued until development approval is granted and its conditions are resolved.

Can I have a building permit without development approval?

Yes, if your project meets the deemed-to-comply rules and doesn't need planning approval. Many patios, sheds and granny flats only need a building permit.

How long does development approval take in WA?

Councils usually have 60 days, or 90 days if they need to consult neighbours or other agencies.

How much is development approval in WA?

For work under $50,000 the fee is $147, as councils such as Melville list it. Bigger projects cost more.
