Owner-builder vs registered builder in WA

Short answer

Both are legal in WA. An owner-builder saves the builder's margin but runs the job, needs state approval first (usually 6 to 8 weeks) and stays responsible for faults for 7 years. A registered builder carries that risk for you. You need one or the other for permit work over $20,000 ($50,000 for sheds and carports).

Last updated 23 November 2026

The short version

In WA, building work that needs a permit and is worth over $20,000 (or $50,000 for sheds, carports and garages) must be done by a registered builder or by you as an approved owner-builder. Both are legal. The difference is who carries the risk.

When owner-building makes sense

  • You have time to run the job and are happy to manage trades.
  • You plan to live in the home (or use the shed or carport yourself) and not sell or rent it straight away. This is a rule for owner-builders.
  • You have done an owner-builder course, or already have building industry registration, plus a white card.

When a registered builder is the better pick

  • You may sell within 7 years. Owner-builders stay responsible for faults and must arrange home indemnity insurance before selling.
  • The land is owned by a company or trust. Owner-builders must be individuals.
  • You have owner-built in the last 6 years and don't have a waiver.
  • You need the work started soon, and can't wait 6 to 8 weeks for owner-builder approval.

What doesn't change

Either way you still need a building permit, and planning approval if the work doesn't meet the R-Codes. Licensed work like electrical and plumbing must still be done by licensed trades. Hiring trades doesn't take away an owner-builder's legal duties.

Questions

Common questions

Is it cheaper to be an owner-builder in WA?

It can be, because you aren't paying a builder's margin. But you take on the builder's risks, including fixing faults for at least 7 years, and you must manage every trade yourself.

How long does owner-builder approval take in WA?

Usually about 6 to 8 weeks. Once it's granted, you have 6 months to apply for your building permit.

Can I sell a house I owner-built?

Yes, but if you sell within 7 years of the building permit, you need home indemnity insurance that covers the buyer for the rest of that 7 years.

How often can I be an owner-builder?

Usually once every 6 years. You can apply to have that rule waived.

Do I need owner-builder approval for a shed?

Only if a permit is needed and the work is worth more than $50,000. Below that, you can usually build it yourself with just a building permit.
