Cost guide

How much does retrospective approval cost in WA?

Short answer

The council fee for a building approval certificate (BA13) is 0.38% of the value of the work, minimum $121, plus a Building Services Levy of 0.274%. You also pay a private building surveyor to inspect the work, and often a drafter and engineer. Both state rates are higher than for a permit taken out before building.

Last updated 10 November 2026

What you pay for

In WA you can't get a building permit after the work is built. Instead you apply for a building approval certificate (form BA13 for houses, sheds, patios, carports and other Class 1 and 10 work). The cost comes in four parts:

The state also sets the value used for these fees: for unauthorised work, it's the current value of the work as decided by the council, not what you paid.

Add the levy on top. Some councils also charge extra items such as a verge bond or inspection fees, so check your council's fee schedule.

The cost that can hurt most

The fees are small next to the cost of changes. If the surveyor finds the work doesn't meet the building rules, you may need to fix it before the certificate is issued. A council can also refuse the application and ask for the work to be removed. Fines for building without a permit can reach $50,000 for a first offence.

Questions

Common questions

How much does retrospective approval cost in WA?

The council fee for a building approval certificate (BA13) is 0.38% of the value of the work, with a minimum of $121. The Building Services Levy is 0.274%. On top of that you pay a private building surveyor for the inspection and certificate (BA18), and often a drafter and engineer.

Is retrospective approval more expensive than a normal permit?

Yes. The council fee is 0.38% instead of 0.32% for an uncertified permit, the levy rate is double (0.274% instead of 0.137%), and you also need a surveyor to inspect the finished work.

What value is used to work out the fee?

For unauthorised work, the state says it's the current value of the work as decided by the council.

Can a council refuse retrospective approval?

Yes. If the work can't be shown to meet the building standards, the council can refuse and ask for changes or removal.
